Beneath the moon, the old trees stand,
Their roots sunk deep beneath the land.
They watched you come, they watched you leave,
They watched you learn what not to believe.

You wanted back the way things were,
Back to the path, back to the blur,
Back to the days before the rot,
Back to a life you somehow forgot.
But trees do not bend the world behind,
Nor grow a second spring on command.
Their rings remember every year.
They keep what happened. You keep running here.

You saw the roots and called them chains,
Saw the branches and called them pains,
Saw the forest darken overhead
And wished the woods would make you less afraid.
You wanted leaves to hide the sky,
Wanted the old ones not to die,
Wanted the bark to stay unscarred,
Wanted the forest to reset its heart.

What a convenient little dream.
As though the axe can leave no seam.
As though a fallen tree can rise
Because you finally changed your mind.
As though the stump beneath the snow
Has any reason to pretend you know
What growing means, what waiting costs,
Or how much forest you already lost.

The oldest trees have seen you stare,
Seen you beg the empty air.
Their branches creak. Their roots remain.
They do not pity you for pain.
They do not whisper, "It's okay."
They do not promise yesterday.
They do not move because you plead.
They simply grow where you would flee.

You had the woods. You had the shade.
You had the paths that you had made.
You let them choke beneath the weeds,
Then blamed the forest for your needs.
You watched the branches split and fall,
Then acted shocked that leaves were gone at all.
You called surrender common sense,
Then called the ruins consequence.

And now you want to go back home.
You want the roots to leave you alone.
You want the trees to close their eyes,
To hide the dead beneath disguise.
You want fresh bark upon old scars,
Clean branches reaching toward the stars.
You want the forest as it stood
Before you learned you damaged wood.

But look around.
The trees are here.
The roots are here.
The rings are here.
Every year you thought was gone
Is buried underneath the bark.
Every branch you left to break
Still hangs there in the dark.

You cannot ask a tree to forget
Which branches snapped beneath the weight.
You cannot ask the forest floor
To give back what it held before.
You cannot walk into the wood,
Point at the trees and say you should
Be standing somewhere else instead.
The forest has no use for it.

So go ahead.
Take one last look.
Count every trunk you overlooked.
Count every root beneath your feet.
Count every place you chose retreat.
Count every ring, every scar,
Every tree that watched you get this far.

They are not cheering.
They are not kind.
They are not waiting to change their mind.
The trees will stand when you are gone.
The forest will keep carrying on.
Branch after branch.
Ring after ring.
Season after season.
Without fixing anything.

And still you came here asking for
The same old path, the same old door.
You want the world to play along,
To act as though you were never wrong.
You want the trees to call you back.
You want the roots to clear the path.
You want the dead leaves swept away
So you can pretend they fell today.

They will not.

The forest remembers.
The roots remember.
The bark remembers.
The rings remember.
And if you still insist on turning back,
Then turn.
Walk toward the old path.
Walk beneath the old trees.
Tell yourself everything can be normal again.

Go on.

See how far the forest lets you.
